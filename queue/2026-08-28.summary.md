# Layer8Culture — Friday, August 28, 2026

Three things landed in the AI news this week and all three are really about the same
thing: who is holding the controls. OpenAI open-sourced the Codex harness under Apache
2.0 — the actual agent runtime behind Codex CLI, with tool execution, cross-session
memory, approval checkpoints and sandbox policy, plus an SDK and app-server. OpenAI
reports the harness alone lifted GPT-5.6 Sol on ARC-AGI-3 from 13.3% to 38.3% while
cutting token use around 6x, which means the scaffolding was most of the result and it
just stopped being anybody's advantage. Slack Code shipped code channels, where you tag
an agent into a conversation and the whole team watches it work — and nothing merges
until a human in the channel approves it, after which the channel archives itself as an
audit log. And OpenAI's Apple Messages plugin for Mac now reads and sends your iMessages
in exchange for Full Disk Access, Contacts and Automation.

Underneath it is last night's Gabe Coleman episode, mined for two moments nobody has
used yet: the drift problem (he uploads his own headshot and a few generations later it
is a stranger wearing his outfit — fixed with project sources, on the free tier), and
the line that quietly reframed the show — you don't need the thousand likes, you need
the few screens where somebody learns something.
