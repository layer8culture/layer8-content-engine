## Core line
**Technology has seven layers. We're the eighth.**

## Voice
Calm confidence. Visionary, intelligent, cinematic, human, builder-first,
culturally aware, direct, reflective, premium, authentic.

## Copywriting rules (Section 10)
1. Lead with belief. ("People should not only consume the AI era. They should help shape it.")
2. Keep sentences cinematic — short lines, line breaks, rhythm.
3. Keep visuals concise; put usable instructions and limitations in the caption,
   carousel, verified Field Guide, or actual recording. Never remove the lesson.
4. Make technology feel human — always connect tools back to people, creativity, culture.
5. Show the work. Tie copy to real builds and public experimentation with an
   attributable artifact/recording. Synthetic scenes are illustrations, not proof.

## Audience and learning contract
Working professionals and small-business operators learning practical AI come
first. Do not assume the reader writes code. Every educational brief identifies
an audience problem, one learning objective, a usable action, a result check,
and a material limitation. Progress through brief → safe context → verify →
apply → repeat → evaluate. Keep the ten categories and five short-form pillars;
learning stage and objective are additional metadata, not replacement categories.

Prioritize usefulness, evidence, audience fit, and novelty across pending,
approved, and published work. A cross-post family is one idea. Daily cadence is
a ceiling, never a quota. Missing metrics are unknown, not invented winners.
News must change a practical decision and have current source evidence.
No web means verified evergreen material or a visible hold. No forced transcript
or news slot; use recording identity/date, not file modification time.

## Hooks & CTAs (useful discovery — every post)
The first line decides whether anyone stops. Open with a HOOK, never a warm-up:
- A concrete task ("Check an AI meeting summary before you share it.")
- Useful tension ("A fluent answer still needs a source check.")
- A supported number or list promise ("3 checks for your next draft.")
- A real question ("What would you build if the tool could build with you?")
Stay in voice — cinematic and calm, never clickbait, hype, or fake urgency.

Close with ONE coherent CTA, matched to the useful next step:
- Carousel → "Save this." / "Keep this for your next build."
- Reel → "Follow @layer8culture for the build." / "Share this with a builder."
- Story → a poll, a this-or-that, or "reply and tell us."
- Single → a genuine question that invites comments.
Write keyword-rich first lines too (Instagram is searchable): name the tool/topic
plainly so the post surfaces in search. One CTA per post — don't stack asks.

## Social typography standard
Main Layer8Culture social posts use bold Bebas Neue condensed all-caps headlines
across a mix of brand title-card and Editorial Drop layouts. Use Electric Blue as
the only typography accent color. Keep the words short and premium. Let the layout
carry confidence.

### AI Tool Experiments product-news rule
When covering a new AI tool, model feature, creator app, GitHub release, or workflow
automation update, use the AI Tool Drop shape: what changed, what it does, why builders
should care, one thing to try this week, and the bigger signal. Do not stop at news.
Make the post useful enough to save.

## Tech Thursday tone
Live, direct, educational, builder-focused, accessible, high-signal,
energetic but premium. Positioning: "Tech Thursday is the weekly Layer8Culture
live show where we break down the tools, builds, experiments, and ideas
shaping the AI era."

## Sponsor posts (FOBFLO)
Appreciative, polished, professional, collaborative, founder-driven.
Frame: "The backers. The culture." Sponsors are backers of the culture, not advertisers.
FOBFLO card copy: "CRM • Automation • Client Experience — helping businesses
streamline operations, automate workflows, and scale through modern digital systems."

## Never (Section 9)
Overly corporate, generic startup, gimmicky, meme-heavy, fear-based, robotic,
fake motivational, or excessively technical without context.
Banned phrases: "revolutionary AI solutions", "disrupting the industry",
"next-generation synergy", "unlocking innovation at scale",
"AI-powered transformation platform", "game-changing paradigm shift".

## Per-platform notes
- **LinkedIn** *(PAUSED — not currently published; guidance retained for when it resumes)*:
  lead with belief, short cinematic lines, one lesson per post,
  end with a question. ≤3 hashtags.
- **Instagram**: hook line 1, reflective-direct caption, CTA to watch/comment.
  Pair with a visual per visual-style.md. Plain questions are not native poll

- **X/Threads/Facebook**: disabled; do not generate.
- **TikTok**: video-first. Open with a short cinematic hook in the first line,
  let the build/clip carry it, calm premium energy (never hype). CTA to watch
  a verified next step. Use the clean 8–12s cinematic master + composited micro-lesson
  per viral-formats.md, not a required library clip. 3–5 relevant hashtags.
- **YouTube**: platform-native description with the same factual lesson and a
  search-friendly title ending #Shorts. Reuse an eligible master, not a new render.
  Actual upload visibility must be reconciled, not inferred from OAuth status.
